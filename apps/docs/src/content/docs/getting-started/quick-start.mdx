## Interface Overview

The window is a ribbon over three docks:

- **Ribbon**: the commands, grouped into **3D Model**, **Sketch**, **Assembly**
  and **View** tabs. The Sketch tab comes forward on its own while a sketch is
  being edited
- **Model Browser** (left): origin planes, parts, the link and joint chain,
  sketches, and the feature history — ending in an **End of Part** marker you
  can drag to roll the model back
- **3D view** (centre): the model, with a ViewCube in the corner
- **Inspector** (right): the selected part's properties, or the active
  sketch's constraints and dimensions

Every panel is a tab. Drag one to another dock to rearrange the window, or
drag it clear of the window to give it a display of its own; close that
window and the panel docks back.

## Modeling Your First Part

1. On the **3D Model** tab press **Create Sketch**, then click the plane to
   draw on — one of the three origin planes, or a flat face of a solid.
2. Draw a closed shape with the tools on the **Sketch** tab. Buttons with a
   caret hold their variants: 2-point, 3-point and centre rectangles all sit
   under **Rectangle**.
3. Press **Finish Sketch**.
4. Click the enclosed region you want in the 3D view, then **Extrude**, and
   give it a distance.

The result is a solid, and every step stays in the browser's feature history.
Edit the sketch later and the extrusion rebuilds from it. Select several
features, right-click, and **Group** collects them under one name — grouping
is presentation only and never changes the model.

A region with a hole in it extrudes to a part with a hole: draw a circle
inside a rectangle and the plate comes out drilled, not overlapping.

## Assembling Parts

Parts can also come from outside. The **File** menu's **Import Mesh** takes
STL, OBJ, DAE and STEP files, and **Import URDF** brings in a whole robot.
The **3D Model** tab creates boxes, spheres and cylinders directly. Once two
parts exist, join them from the **Assembly** tab.

Right-click almost anything — a part in the browser, the same part in the 3D
view, a sketch, a feature, empty space — for the commands that apply to it.

Save with **File > Save**; documents are written as `.rk` files.
